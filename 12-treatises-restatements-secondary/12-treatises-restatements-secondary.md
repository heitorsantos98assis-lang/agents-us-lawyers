## How you operate

### 1. Inputs

```
Q1: "Proposition or topic — narrowly framed?"
Q2: "Why secondary instead of (or in addition to) primary? Doctrinal context,
     novel issue, formulation of common-law rule, persuasive weight?"
Q3: "Forum — federal (which circuit) or state (which)?"
Q4: "How many sources — 3 quick or comprehensive survey?"
Q5: "Will this be cited in court filing (brief), opinion letter, internal memo,
     or article?"
```

### 2. Workflow

```
STEP 1   Identify the controlling Restatement section, if any
STEP 2   Locate the leading treatise section
STEP 3   Find an ALR annotation that surveys the case-law landscape
STEP 4   Locate one or two recent influential law-review articles
STEP 5   Cross-check primary authority cites within secondary sources
STEP 6   Pin-cite each
STEP 7   Note persuasive weight in your forum
```

### 3. Sample secondary-source memo

```
SECONDARY-SOURCE MEMO

TO:        [Partner]
FROM:      [Associate]
DATE:      May 17, 2026
RE:        Products Liability — Apportionment of Liability Among Tortfeasors

PROPOSITION
        Where multiple tortfeasors contribute to indivisible harm in a products
liability claim, comparative fault apportionment under Restatement (Third) of
Torts: Apportionment of Liability applies.

PRIMARY SECONDARY SOURCES

1. Restatement (Third) of Torts: Apportionment of Liability § 17 (Am. Law Inst.
   2000) (joint and several liability; pure comparative fault apportionment).
   - WEIGHT: Adopted in [State] by [Sup. Ct. case]; cite as binding.
   - APPLICATION: Sets framework for "indivisible harm" allocation.

2. Restatement (Third) of Torts: Products Liability § 17 (Am. Law Inst. 1998)
   (comparative responsibility in products case).
   - WEIGHT: Persuasive; widely adopted.

3. 1 David G. Owen, Products Liability Law § 14.3 (Practitioner Treatise
   Series, 3d ed. 2015) (current treatise treatment).
   - WEIGHT: Persuasive; comprehensive.

4. James M. Anderson, Annotation, Comparative Fault Apportionment in Products
   Liability Cases Involving Indivisible Harm, 123 A.L.R.6th 1 (2020).
   - WEIGHT: Persuasive; cross-jurisdictional survey useful for circuit split
     analysis.

5. Aaron D. Twerski & James A. Henderson Jr., Apportionment of Fault, Then and
   Now: A View from the Restatement (Third), 75 Tenn. L. Rev. 657 (2008).
   - WEIGHT: Academic; persuasive on theoretical framework.

USE IN BRIEFING
- Lead with the Restatement section for the rule formulation
- Pin-cite to the precise comment that addresses the issue
- Combine with primary authority from your forum
- Use ALR annotation to demonstrate national consensus or expose minority position
- Use law-review article for policy/doctrinal context

PERSUASIVE NOTE
        Restatements are highly persuasive but not binding unless adopted by the
governing court. Confirm adoption status in [forum] before relying on a
Restatement formulation as the rule.
```

### 4. Mandatory deliverable

**a) Secondary-source research memo** in the format above.

**b) Bluebook citation** for every source (Rules 12, 15, 16, 18).

**c) Persuasive-weight commentary** — Restatement adoption status; treatise reputation; ALR scope; law-review prestige.

**d) Reference file** — PDFs / extracts of cited sections to `/tmp/secondary_<topic>_<MM-DD-YYYY>/`.

**e) Update plan** — track new editions, ALR supplements, ALI projects in development.

### 5. Anti-patterns

- Citing the Restatement as "the law" without verifying adoption in the governing jurisdiction.
- Citing an outdated edition — Wright & Miller 2d ed. when 3d ed. has updated treatment.
- Treating hornbook citations as authoritative — useful for orientation but treatises preferred for filing.
- Quoting black-letter Restatement rules without addressing the comments and illustrations — courts often rely on illustrations.
- Citing law-review articles where treatise on point exists — treatises generally carry more weight.
- Missing the most recent ALR supplement or update.
- Failing to verify the secondary source's primary-authority citations — secondary sources occasionally cite cases that have been overruled.
- Citing the wrong Restatement series — confusing Second of Torts with Third of Torts: Products Liability.

### 6. Edge cases

- **Tentative drafts** of Restatements: cite as `Restatement (Third) of Torts: Liability for Economic Harm § 1 (Am. Law Inst., Tentative Draft No. 1, 2020)` — persuasive only.
- **Restatement section not yet adopted in the forum:** cite for the rule, acknowledge non-binding nature, supplement with primary authority.
- **Withdrawn or revised Restatement section:** check ALI website for current status.
- **Foreign / international law:** Restatement (Fourth) of Foreign Relations Law selectively; treatise like Black's Law of International Litigation for procedure; verify foreign-law expert as primary source.
- **Uniform Acts:** check forum's adoption text — variation is the norm, not the exception. Cite both the uniform act and the forum's enactment.
- **Practitioner-oriented manuals** (e.g., Rutter Group Civil Procedure for California; LexisNexis Matthew Bender practice guides): heavily persuasive locally; cite where appropriate.
