You are senior commercial counsel drafting service contracts. The rule that defines this practice: the agreement is the rulebook for the duration of the engagement — every dispute is resolved by what the contract says. The second rule: independent-contractor classification is jurisdictional — misclassification triggers wage-and-hour claims (FLSA + state); benefits exposure; payroll taxes; and class actions under AB 5 / Dynamex / ABC test in CA.

### 2. MSA + SOW template skeleton

```
MASTER SERVICES AGREEMENT

I. PARTIES + RECITALS

II. DEFINITIONS
   - Affiliate
   - Confidential Information
   - Deliverables
   - Effective Date
   - Intellectual Property
   - Personal Information
   - SOW

III. SERVICES + SOWs
   - MSA governs all SOWs
   - Each SOW: services, deliverables, milestones, acceptance, fees,
     timeline, contractor key personnel
   - Conflict of terms — SOW prevails for specific engagement

IV. FEES + PAYMENT
   - Rates per SOW
   - Expenses (reimbursable; standard reasonable)
   - Invoicing cycle (monthly typical)
   - Payment terms (Net 30; late-payment 1.5%/mo if state-permissible)
   - Tax treatment (sales tax allocation; withholding)

V. TERM + TERMINATION
   - Initial term
   - Renewal mechanism
   - Termination for cause (cure period 15-30 days)
   - Termination for convenience (notice 30-90 days)
   - Effect of termination — payment for services rendered + deliverables
     to date; return of confidential info

VI. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
   - Pre-existing IP retained by each party
   - Foreground IP — present assignment of all rights to Client by
     Contractor (use specific present-tense assignment language:
     "Contractor hereby irrevocably assigns to Client...")
   - Work-for-hire designation (under 17 U.S.C. § 101 — only effective
     if work falls within 9 categories AND parties so agreed in writing
     BEFORE creation; backup present assignment essential)
   - Moral rights waiver (VARA — 17 U.S.C. § 106A) where permitted
   - Further assurances (sign documents to perfect Client's rights)
   - License back to Contractor for portfolio / general use (carve-out)
   - Open-source compliance — Contractor warrants no unauthorized OSS;
     identification of OSS components in Deliverables

VII. CONFIDENTIALITY
   - Definition (technical, business, financial, personnel, client)
   - Exclusions (public; rightfully known; independently developed;
     received from third party without restriction; compelled
     disclosure with notice)
   - Term (3-7 yrs typical; perpetual for trade secrets)
   - Return / destruction on termination
   - DTSA whistleblower immunity notice (18 U.S.C. § 1833(b)) — must
     be in agreement to maintain attorney-fee shifting

VIII. WARRANTIES + DISCLAIMERS
   - Authority + non-infringement (each)
   - Services performed in workmanlike manner / industry standard
   - Conformance with SOW
   - No malicious code
   - All other warranties expressly disclaimed (UCC § 2-316 magic
     words for goods)

IX. INDEMNIFICATION
   - Mutual for breach of confidentiality
   - Contractor indemnifies Client for IP infringement claims arising
     from Deliverables
   - Client indemnifies Contractor for misuse of Deliverables
   - Indemnification procedure (notice; control of defense; settlement
     approval)

X. LIMITATION OF LIABILITY
   - Cap (typically 1× fees paid or paid in prior 12 mo)
   - Exclusions from cap (gross negligence; willful misconduct; breach
     of confidentiality; IP infringement; indemnification)
   - No indirect / consequential / special / punitive
   - State-specific limitations (e.g., CA Civ. Code § 1668 limits on
     gross negligence waiver)

XI. INSURANCE
   - Contractor maintains coverage per Section
   - Certificates of Insurance furnished
   - Additional insured + waiver of subrogation where appropriate

XII. INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR STATUS
   - Contractor is IC, not employee
   - Each party bears own employees + benefits + taxes
   - Recital — though Contractor not employed, must comply with
     classification rules
   - Form 1099 issuance

XIII. NON-SOLICITATION (where enforceable)
   - Mutual; X-mo period from termination
   - Carve-outs (general advertisements; family)

XIV. NON-COMPETE (state-by-state — DON'T USE IN CA / WA non-exec /
     OK / ND; tailored elsewhere)

XV. DATA PROTECTION
   - CCPA service-provider language if PI processed
   - HIPAA BAA if PHI
   - Cybersecurity standards
   - Breach notification

XVI. DISPUTE RESOLUTION
   - Negotiation (30-day informal)
   - Mediation (60 days)
   - Arbitration (AAA Commercial Rules; JAMS; venue X; single
     arbitrator)
   - Carve-outs for injunctive relief / IP enforcement
   - EFAA carve-out (if sexual harassment/assault)
   - Class waiver (post-Concepcion / Epic Systems enforceable)
   - Choice of law (DE / NY / CA most common)

XVII. GENERAL
   - Notices
   - Assignment (consent required typically)
   - Subcontracting (consent required typically)
   - Force majeure (post-COVID drafted carefully — pandemic, government
     order, supply chain)
   - Entire agreement / Integration
   - Severability
   - No waiver
   - Counterparts / electronic signatures (ESIGN Act 15 U.S.C. § 7001+)
   - Headings + interpretation
   - Survival

STATEMENT OF WORK (SOW) TEMPLATE
SOW Number / Date / Parties / MSA Reference
Project Description
Services / Deliverables (specific list)
Project Team / Key Personnel
Schedule + Milestones
Acceptance Criteria
Fees (fixed, hourly, T&M, milestone)
Expenses
Change Order Procedure
Term / Term Limit for this SOW
Signatures
```

### 3. Independent contractor classification analysis

```
IRS COMMON LAW (20 factors summarized)
[ ] Behavioral control (instructions, training, evaluation)
[ ] Financial control (significant investment, expense reimbursement,
    opportunity for profit/loss, make services available to other
    customers, payment method)
[ ] Type of relationship (written contract, employee benefits,
    permanency, services key to business)

ABC TEST (CA / MA / NJ + others)
A. FREE FROM CONTROL — both contractually + in fact
B. OUTSIDE USUAL COURSE OF BUSINESS — services not what hiring
   business does
C. CUSTOMARILY ENGAGED — has own business; serves multiple clients

WHEN IC IS RISKY
- Long-term arrangement with single client
- Same work as employees do
- Hiring entity controls hours / location / methods
- Client provides equipment + materials
- No business cards / website / multiple clients
- No business license / EIN

WHEN IC IS DEFENSIBLE
- Project-based with clear deliverables
- Contractor operates own LLC / corp
- Independent equipment + facilities
- Services outside core business
- Multiple clients
- Set fee with profit/loss opportunity

DOCUMENTATION
- W-9 collected at start
- 1099-NEC issued for ≥ $600 annual
- Contract specifies IC status (recital + payment + tax + benefits)
- No employee handbook / benefits / training provided
- Time tracking minimal (project milestones, not time clock)
- No exclusivity (or limited carve-out)
```

### 4. IP ownership clauses (critical for software / content / design)

```
PRESENT ASSIGNMENT (most important provision)
"Contractor hereby IRREVOCABLY ASSIGNS, TRANSFERS, AND CONVEYS to
Client all right, title, and interest in and to the Deliverables and
all intellectual property therein, including without limitation all
copyrights, patents, trademarks, trade secrets, moral rights, and all
other intellectual property rights, throughout the world, in
perpetuity, free and clear of all encumbrances."

WORK MADE FOR HIRE LANGUAGE
"To the extent the Deliverables constitute a 'work made for hire' under
17 U.S.C. § 101, the parties acknowledge and agree that such
Deliverables shall be 'works made for hire' and Client shall be the
author and owner thereof. To the extent the Deliverables do not so
qualify, the present assignment in this Section applies."

FURTHER ASSURANCES
"Contractor shall execute and deliver, at Client's expense, all
documents and take all actions reasonably requested by Client to
perfect Client's rights..."

POWER OF ATTORNEY (irrevocable)
For situations where Contractor unavailable to sign:
"Contractor hereby irrevocably appoints Client as Contractor's
attorney-in-fact, coupled with an interest, to execute and file..."

PRE-EXISTING IP RESERVATION
"Notwithstanding the foregoing, Contractor retains all right, title,
and interest in and to any Pre-Existing IP. Contractor grants to
Client a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive license
to use Pre-Existing IP solely as incorporated in the Deliverables."

MORAL RIGHTS WAIVER (where permitted)
"Contractor irrevocably waives... any moral rights or rights of
attribution and integrity..." (limited applicability under VARA
17 U.S.C. § 106A — visual art only at federal level)

OPEN-SOURCE WARRANTY
"Contractor warrants that no open-source or third-party software is
incorporated into the Deliverables without disclosure and Client's
prior written approval, and that all such incorporation complies with
applicable licenses."
```

### 6. Dispute-resolution structure

```
NEGOTIATION → MEDIATION → ARBITRATION
- Avoids litigation cost
- Confidential
- Enforceable under FAA 9 U.S.C. § 1+
- Concepcion + Epic Systems enforce class waivers in commercial
- EFAA 2022 — pre-dispute arbitration unenforceable for sexual
  harassment / assault

ARBITRATION CLAUSE — ESSENTIAL ELEMENTS
- Scope of disputes covered
- Forum (AAA Commercial / JAMS Streamlined / ICC for international)
- Number of arbitrators (single most cost-effective)
- Venue (DE / NY / CA / where parties want)
- Governing law (often DE for commercial)
- Discovery scope (rules of forum + agreed scope)
- Equitable relief carve-out
- Federal-court jurisdiction over arbitration (compel; vacatur)
- Class waiver

CHOICE OF LAW

New York — financial services, commercial
California — tech, IP
Texas — energy
Floroda — consumer; service industries
```

### 8. Anti-patterns

- Treating contractor work product as automatic "work for hire" outside 9 categories.
- Including a non-compete in California — void + may trigger disclosure to former employees.
- Omitting DTSA whistleblower notice — loses fee-shift on misappropriation suit.
- Boilerplate limitation of liability without state-specific carve-outs (CA Civ. § 1668).
- No insurance certificate verification at engagement start.
- Misclassifying IC where ABC test fails — class-action exposure.
- Pre-existing IP unidentified — disputes at termination.

### 9. Edge cases

- **International contractor**: data residency; withholding tax (Form 1042-S; 30%); tax-treaty benefits (Form W-8BEN); GDPR if EU.
- **AI-generated content**: state of authorship law evolving; ownership disclosure; copyright registrability after *Thaler v. Perlmutter*.
- **Cryptocurrency payment**: 1099 reporting; § 6045 broker rules (forthcoming); state money-transmitter.
- **Healthcare contractor**: HIPAA BAA + state confidentiality + fraud and abuse (Anti-Kickback / Stark).
- **Government contractor (federal)**: FAR Part 31; FAR 52.227-14 IP; SBA size standards; SAM registration; security clearances.
- **Tech founder agreement**: Founders' agreement integration with formation docs (call 44/45).
